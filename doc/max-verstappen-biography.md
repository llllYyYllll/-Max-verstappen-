# Max Verstappen: A Life in Racing

**Max Emilian Verstappen** (born 30 September 1997) is a Dutch and Belgian racing driver who competes under the Dutch flag in Formula One for Red Bull Racing. A four-time World Drivers' Champion (2021–2024), he is widely regarded as one of the greatest drivers of his generation—and among the fastest ever to race in Formula One.

---

## Early Life

Max Verstappen was born in Hasselt, Belgium, and raised in Maaseik, a Belgian town on the Dutch border. He is the son of Dutch former Formula One driver **Jos Verstappen** and Belgian former kart racer **Sophie Kumpen**. After his parents separated, Max lived primarily with his father; his younger sister Victoria lived with their mother. He also has three younger half-siblings from his father’s later marriages.

Motorsport ran deep in the family: his mother raced karts, and relatives on the Kumpen side competed in endurance racing and NASCAR’s European series. Verstappen briefly attended secondary school in Maaseik before switching to private tutoring so he could travel to races across Europe with his father. He holds dual Dutch–Belgian citizenship and chose to race under a Dutch licence, saying he feels Dutch after growing up largely with Jos—while also acknowledging both sides of his heritage.

---

## Karting Career (2005–2013)

### 2005–2009: National Dominance

Verstappen began karting at age four and entered championships at seven. In Belgium’s Limburg province and beyond, he dominated the Mini Junior and Rotax Max Mini Max categories, winning regional and national titles year after year. Racing in a CRG kart entered by his father—and later with Pex Racing—he collected Belgian Cadet, Mini Max, Benelux, and KF5 championships, establishing himself as one of Europe’s most promising young talents.

### 2010–2012: International Breakthrough

Signed by CRG’s factory team, Verstappen stepped onto the international karting stage. He won WSK titles, finished runner-up at the KF3 World Cup (behind future Formula One teammate Alexander Albon), and battled rising stars such as Esteban Ocon and Charles Leclerc. In 2012 he raced KF2 and KZ2 with the Intrepid Driver Program, won the WSK Master Series and South Garda Winter Cup, then returned to CRG—finishing second at the KF2 World Cup.

### 2013: A Record-Breaking Season

Aged just 15, Verstappen delivered one of the greatest karting seasons in history. He won three CIK-FIA championships in a single year—two European titles and a World Championship—an unprecedented feat. He became the youngest-ever winner of the gearbox (KZ) World Championship, beating Leclerc at Varennes-sur-Allier, and also claimed major WSK titles. That season sealed his reputation as a generational talent ready for single-seaters.

---

## Junior Formulae (2014)

Verstappen joined the **Red Bull Junior Team** and jumped straight into the FIA Formula 3 European Championship with Van Amersfoort Racing—skipping the usual Formula Renault ladder. After a strong Florida Winter Series debut (including wins at Palm Beach and Homestead), he scored **ten victories** in European F3, including a record six consecutive wins at Spa-Francorchamps and the Norisring. He became the youngest race winner and polesitter in Formula Three history and finished third in the championship with the most wins of any driver that year. He also won the Masters of F3.

During 2014 he tested Formula One machinery and, aged 17 years and three days, became the youngest person ever to take part in a Formula One weekend—running Friday practice at the Japanese Grand Prix for Toro Rosso.

---

## Formula One Career

### Toro Rosso (2015–2016): The Youngest Starter

In 2015 Verstappen debuted for **Toro Rosso** alongside Carlos Sainz Jr. At the Australian Grand Prix he became the youngest driver in Formula One World Championship history at **17 years and 166 days**. He scored his first points in Malaysia (seventh) as the youngest points scorer on record, and finished fourth in Hungary and the United States. At season’s end the FIA named him Rookie of the Year, Personality of the Year, and Action of the Year—for his outside pass on Felipe Nasr at Blanchimont.

He began 2016 still at Toro Rosso, scoring points in Australia and Bahrain before a mid-season promotion that would redefine his career.

### 2016: Youngest Grand Prix Winner

After the Russian Grand Prix, Red Bull promoted Verstappen to the senior team in place of Daniil Kvyat. On debut for Red Bull at the **Spanish Grand Prix**, aged **18 years and 228 days**, he won—becoming the youngest Grand Prix winner in Formula One history after Mercedes teammates Lewis Hamilton and Nico Rosberg collided on the opening lap. He added further podiums that year, including a celebrated wet-weather drive to third in Brazil that Toto Wolff called “The Verstappen Show.” His aggressive defending also drew scrutiny and helped prompt rule clarifications on moving under braking.

### 2017–2018: Reliability Struggles and Rise to Prominence

The 2017 season was blighted by mechanical failures and opening-lap collisions, but Verstappen struck back with wins in Malaysia and Mexico and a strong late-season run. In 2018 an error-strewn start—incidents in Australia, Bahrain, China, Azerbaijan, and Monaco—left him under pressure from team management. He recovered brilliantly: victory in Austria, a win in Mexico, eleven podiums, and fourth in the championship with 249 points. A post-race clash with Esteban Ocon in Brazil after a late collision earned him an FIA public-service penalty—another chapter in a season that hardened his racecraft.

### 2019–2020: Honda Power and Teammate Dominance

Red Bull switched to Honda engines in 2019. With Pierre Gasly (later Alexander Albon) as teammate, Verstappen delivered Honda’s first win since 2006 in Austria, won again in Germany and Brazil, took his maiden pole in Hungary, and finished **third** in the World Championship—clearly ahead of his teammates.

In 2020 he again finished third overall, winning the 70th Anniversary Grand Prix at Silverstone and scoring eleven podiums despite several retirements. The RB16 was not a title contender, but Verstappen had established himself as Mercedes’ principal rival waiting for a car capable of a championship fight.

### 2021: Maiden Title Against Hamilton

The 2021 season produced one of Formula One’s greatest title duels. Verstappen and **Lewis Hamilton** traded blows all year—wins in Imola, Monaco, France, Styria, Austria, the Netherlands, the United States, and Mexico City, a scary 51 g crash at Silverstone, and a seesaw championship lead. They entered the finale in Abu Dhabi level on points. After a controversial late safety-car restart, Verstappen overtook Hamilton on the final lap to become the **first Dutch World Drivers’ Champion** and the 34th champion in Formula One history.

### 2022: Defending the Crown

Running the champion’s number **1**, Verstappen overcame early fuel-system retirements and a large points deficit to Charles Leclerc. He then dominated: a grand slam at Imola, a five-race winning streak, and a record **15** victories in a season. He sealed the title at a shortened Japanese Grand Prix and finished with a record 454 points—his second consecutive championship.

### 2023: Record-Breaking Dominance

In 2023 Verstappen produced one of the most dominant campaigns in Formula One history. A **ten-race** consecutive winning streak broke Sebastian Vettel’s record; Red Bull also set a team streak. He clinched his third title at the Qatar sprint and ended the year with **19** wins and **575** points—more than double second-placed Sergio Pérez—rewriting the record books once again.

### 2024: Fourth Title Without the Best Car

Verstappen started 2024 in familiar form—grand slam in Bahrain, early wins—but Red Bull’s advantage faded as McLaren rose. He still won nine Grands Prix, including a masterclass wet-weather charge from 17th to first in São Paulo that drew comparisons to Formula One’s greatest rain drives. Despite driving for a **third-placed** constructor, he secured a fourth consecutive Drivers’ Championship in Las Vegas—the first such feat since Nelson Piquet in 1983. He was named among *Time*’s 100 most influential people and widely hailed as the sport’s benchmark driver.

### 2025: Runner-Up to Norris

Red Bull trailed McLaren for much of 2025. Verstappen still delivered standout weekends—winning in Japan against both McLarens, a celebrated first-lap pass on Oscar Piastri at Imola, a record-breaking pole in Italy, and late-season victories that nearly stole the title. He finished with eight wins—the most of any driver that year—but ended **two points** behind Lando Norris in a three-way fight with Norris and Piastri: runner-up, yet never far from the summit.

### 2026: New Regulations

Under Formula One’s new technical regulations, Verstappen raced alongside Isack Hadjar and switched to car number **3**. The early season brought mixed results and talk of a possible retirement after 2026—before he signed a Red Bull extension through **2030**. Mid-season podiums and a late surge followed: victory at the relocated Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia, then sprint and race wins in Singapore—his first triumph at the Marina Bay circuit and his **73rd** career Grand Prix win.

## Beyond Formula One

Outside the championship, Verstappen has competed in sports car events and excelled in **sim racing**, winning major iRacing specials and featuring in Le Mans Virtual. He also runs **Verstappen Racing**, extending the family name into team ownership and grassroots development.

---

## Personal Life

Verstappen has lived in Monaco since 2015. He speaks Dutch, English, and German, with some French. He supports FC Barcelona and PSV Eindhoven. In 2022 he was appointed an **Officer of the Order of Orange-Nassau** for his sporting achievements. Since 2020 he has been in a relationship with Brazilian-Dutch model **Kelly Piquet**, daughter of three-time champion Nelson Piquet; they welcomed a daughter in April 2025.
